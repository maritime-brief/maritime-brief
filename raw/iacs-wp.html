In demonstrating its ongoing commitment to supporting the work of the International Maritime Organization (IMO), and within its remit as the Organization\u2019s principal technical advisor, IACS will actively participate in the forthcoming 12th session of the Sub-Committee on Carriage of Cargoes and Containers (CCC 12) which will meet from 14 to 18 September 2026.<\/strong><\/p>\n

Noting the work IACS Members undertake as recognized organizations of IMO Member States, verifying compliance of ships with IMO agreed requirements, and with a view to achieving clarity for all parties, IACS has submitted the following documents:<\/p>\n

CCC 12\/3\/3<\/a> proposes amendments to the IGF Code to promote uniform implementation of the Code and maintain its safety objectives.<\/p>\n

CCC 12\/4<\/a> proposes interim guidelines for the safety of ships using onboard carbon capture and storage systems (OCCS), in response to gap G 34 identified by MSC 110. While OCCS are considered a potential means to reduce CO\u2082 emissions from ships, their installation and integration introduce additional safety considerations related to ship integration, CO\u2082 processing and storage, and the use of chemical treatment fluids. The proposed interim guidelines adopt a goal based and risk based approach to address these hazards, allowing flexibility to accommodate the wide variety of ship types and OCCS arrangements while ensuring an acceptable level of safety.<\/p>\n

CCC 12\/9<\/a> contains an IACS UI (SC308\/Rev.1), of the requirements for cargo space ventilation, as contained in SOLAS regulation II-2\/5.2.1.1 and in the IMSBC Code, applicable when carrying the new cargo type Direct Reduced Iron (D), in accordance with the amendments to the IMSBC Code (resolution MSC.539(107)).<\/p>\n

CCC 12\/12<\/a> proposes a minor correction to paragraphs 6.7.3.1.1 and 6.7.2.6 of the IGF Code, as amended by resolution MSC.551(108).<\/p>\n

In support of this new framework and to facilitate consistent global implementation of the SOLAS regulations,\u00a0IACS has developed a comprehensive<strong> Recommendation on Onboard Lifting Appliances<\/strong>, providing guidance on, for example:<\/p>\n<ul>\n<li>Distinguishing between new and existing lifting appliances;<\/li>\n<li>Application of SOLAS regulation II-1\/3-13 to lifting appliances with a safe working load below 1000 kg;<\/li>\n<li>What information is the prerequisite to account for existing lifting appliances and loose gear that enters into the SOLAS regime (certificates, load test and thorough examination);<\/li>\n<li>Examples of loose gear to which the application of SOLAS regulation II-1\/3-13 should be considered and for where it should not and the documentation required for the consideration of existing loose gear in the SOLAS regulation II-1\/3-13 regime;<\/li>\n<li>Appliances serving as launching appliances for survival craft or rescue boats and as lifting appliances for cargo handling (dual use);<\/li>\n<li>Necessary documentation and certification procedure for equipment with modification or alteration of major character;<\/li>\n<li>Survey and testing regimes and timeframes, including alignment with the Harmonized System of Survey and Certification (HSSC);<\/li>\n<li>Date of the initial verification of compliance with SOLAS regulation II-1\/3-13 regime.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n<p>Commenting on the new Regulation, Robert Ashdown, IACS Secretary General, said:<\/p>\n<p><em>\u201cAs the new SOLAS requirements take effect, close cooperation among classification societies, Flag State Administrations and industry stakeholders will be essential.\u00a0IACS remains committed to providing clear, practical guidance and supporting the maritime sector in achieving safer lifting operations worldwide, and the new IACS Recommendation will help ensure the consistent application of the SOLAS rules, reduce the risks to crew and vessels of lifting appliance failure.\u201d <\/em><\/p>\n<p>The new IACS Recommendation builds on the SOLAS regulations by providing detailed reference to relevant aspects of SOLAS and the scenarios relating to the various modes of application of lifting appliances.
